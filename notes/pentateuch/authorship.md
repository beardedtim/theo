Mosaic authorship is the traditional Jewish and Christian position, based
on internal claims (e.g. Exodus 24:4, Deuteronomy 31:9) and later
canonical/rabbinic tradition rather than an explicit universal claim of
sole authorship across all five books.

Since Astruc and Wellhausen, the dominant critical model has been the
Documentary Hypothesis: the Pentateuch as a redaction of at least four
originally distinct sources (Yahwist, Elohist, Deuteronomist, Priestly),
identified by divine-name usage, doublets, and stylistic/theological
seams, compiled over a span reaching into the post-exilic period. The
classical JEDP model has been substantially revised (supplementary and
fragmentary hypotheses now compete with it), but the core claim --
composite, multi-period authorship rather than a single Mosaic hand --
remains the scholarly mainstream.

Net: treat authorship as attribute-level metadata inherited by every book
in this group, not a settled fact -- Genesis-Deuteronomy individually may
still carry their own more specific notes where the case differs (e.g.
Deuteronomy's distinct style/vocabulary is itself a major plank of the
source-critical argument).
